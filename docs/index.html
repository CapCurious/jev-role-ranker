What Jev is

Jev is a hosted decision model from TypeSafe. You send a JSON state and typed questions (yes or no, pick one, a 1 to 5 score). It answers with probabilities and no prose, about 0.2 seconds and a few hundredths of a cent per call.

Because the answers are numbers, the policy lives in your code. The weights and cut points above are plain parameters, so changing one re-sorts the table without another inference. This kit asks four narrow questions per posting: does it refuse sponsorship, how well does it fit, how senior is it, how remote is it.

The band rule

Pick the number to band, then cut it twice: at or above act, the item acts; from review up to act, a person looks; below review, it escalates.

For a yes or no question the number is certainty, the larger of p and 1 minus p, so a confident no acts as readily as a confident yes. For a choice or a score it is the returned confidence. Community defaults are 0.85 and 0.60, and each question should get its own cut points sized to the cost of a wrong call.

This ranker is the one exception. It bands the raw probability of a refusal, so a confident "no refusal" stays blank. A flag is a prompt to read the posting, never a rejection.

The limits

  • The postings are synthetic: 80 generated texts with labels by construction, model jev-1.13.0, recorded 2026-10-18. Real postings bury the sponsorship sentence, so expect lower numbers.
  • The three narrow questions match gold on all 80. Fit is the soft one (Spearman 0.665 alone), which is why the overall rank is 0.863 and not 1.
  • On a stratified 40, Claude (sonnet) tracks gold fit slightly better than Jev (0.764 versus 0.709) at about 20 times the latency and over 100 times the cost.
  • No explanations by design. Calibration is a population property: 0.9 means about nine in ten across many items, not that this one is right. Thresholds do not transfer across datasets or model versions.
  • The score only sorts. A blank sponsorship cell does not mean safe.
